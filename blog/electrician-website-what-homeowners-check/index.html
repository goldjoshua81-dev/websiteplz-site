Electrician websites: what homeowners check before they call

By WebsitePlz · October 3, 2026 · 4 min read

Electrical work is one of the jobs homeowners are most careful about. It happens inside their walls, it can be dangerous when done wrong, and larger projects like panel upgrades or EV charger installs aren't cheap. So before they call, they check. Here's what they're looking for and how your website can answer each question in seconds.

"Are you licensed?"

This is usually the first question, and it should be the easiest to answer. Put your license number near the top of the page, in a trust strip or right under your headline, and again in the footer. Show it exactly as it appears on your license. Licensing rules are set by states and sometimes cities, so the format varies, but homeowners who want to verify it should be able to.

If you're insured, say so. If you pull permits and handle inspections for the jobs that need them, say that too. It's a real selling point, because it tells homeowners the work will be done properly.

"Do you do my kind of job?"

Homeowners search for specific jobs: "panel upgrade," "EV charger installation," "outlet not working," "ceiling fan install." A page that just says "residential electrical services" makes them guess. Give your main services their own short sections:

  • Troubleshooting and repairs: dead outlets, tripping breakers, flickering lights.
  • Panel upgrades and service changes, if you do them.
  • EV charger installs, including whether you handle the permit.
  • Lighting, fans and fixtures, or whatever your bread-and-butter work is.

Lead with the jobs you want more of, and leave off anything you don't do. If you do commercial work too, give it its own section so homeowners and businesses both know they're in the right place.

"Can I trust you in my home?"

Reviews and real photos do most of the work here.

  • Real reviews from your customers. A few short ones on your page, with a link to your Google profile for the rest. Never write, buy or reward reviews. The FTC's final rule on fake reviews and testimonials covers businesses that do, and Google's review policies prohibit fake and incentivized reviews.
  • Photos of you and your work. A clean panel you installed, your van, your team. Clear phone photos are fine and beat generic stock.
  • A short "about" line. Who owns the business and how long you've served the area, only if it's accurate.

"What will it cost?"

You don't have to publish prices, and for many electrical jobs you can't until you've seen the work. But you can still lower the worry. Say how you price ("written estimate before any work starts," "flat-rate pricing on common repairs") if that's how you work. If you offer financing for bigger projects, say so and link to the application. Only promise what you actually do.

"Can you come soon, and do you serve my area?"

For urgent problems, say whether you take emergency calls and when. Put a tap-to-call button at the top of the page and keep a call bar on screen as people scroll, so the number is never more than a tap away.

List the cities, towns or counties you serve. If you don't have a storefront, Google lets service-area businesses hide their address and set a service area on their Business Profile. Use the same area on your website.

Make planned projects easy to quote

Bigger jobs often start with a form, not a call. A homeowner thinking about an EV charger may be researching at night after work. Give them a short request form that asks only what you need to follow up: name, phone, address, the type of job and an optional photo of the panel or the spot where the work would go. Every extra field costs you some requests, so keep it short and follow up quickly.

Say what happens next, for example "We'll call to schedule a free estimate." A clear next step makes people more likely to send the form.

Local SEO basics for electricians

Your Google Business Profile is often how homeowners find you, and Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. A good website supports your profile. The basics, all covered in Google's SEO starter guide:

  • A clear page title like "Your Company | Licensed Electrician in Your City."
  • Your services and service area written on the page in normal sentences.
  • Consistent details. The same business name, phone and service area on your site, your Google profile and other listings. Google's profile guidelines say to use your real business name without added keywords.
  • Local business structured data, which Google documents in its local business guide.
  • A fast, mobile-friendly page. Check it free with PageSpeed Insights.
  • Ongoing reviews. Ask happy customers to review you on Google, and reply to the reviews you get.

What to skip

You don't need a huge site. Skip the stock photos of lightning bolts, the pages you won't keep updated and the pop-ups. A single fast page that answers the five questions above, with a call button and a short quote form, is enough for many independent electricians and small shops.

The short version

Homeowners hiring an electrician want proof of a license, a clear list of what you do, a reason to trust you, an idea of how you price, and an easy way to reach you. Put all five on one fast page and you'll win more of the comparisons that happen before the phone rings.
