Pressure washing sells itself with pictures. A grimy driveway next to a clean one does more than any sales pitch. The problem is that most of those pictures live in a Facebook or Instagram feed, where they scroll away and are hard to book from. A simple website fixes that. Here's what it needs to turn interest into booked jobs.

Lead with your before-and-after photos

Your gallery is your strongest sales tool, so put it high on the page, not on a separate page nobody opens. A few tips:

  • Show pairs from the same angle. Before and after side by side, shot from the same spot, makes the difference obvious.
  • Use your own work only. Stock photos or other companies' results mislead customers, and people notice.
  • Cover your main services. A house, a driveway, a deck or fence, a storefront if you do commercial. Customers look for the job they need.
  • Phone photos are fine. Shoot in good light, keep the lens clean and crop out clutter.

Answer "how much?" before they ask

Price is the question you get most in DMs. Your website can handle a lot of it. You have three options, and any of them beats silence:

  • Flat prices for standard jobs, if your pricing is predictable.
  • "Starting at" prices, with a note that final price depends on size and condition.
  • A free-quote form that asks for the address, the surfaces to clean and a couple of photos, so you can quote without a site visit.

Whatever you choose, stick to it. If the site says a price, honor it.

Make the quote button impossible to miss

Put a "Get a Free Quote" button at the top of the page and keep it on screen as people scroll, along with a tap-to-call button. Keep the form short: name, phone or email, address, what needs cleaning, and an optional photo upload or a note to text photos. Tell people when to expect a reply, but only promise a time you can keep.

Explain your services in plain words

Customers don't always know the difference between pressure washing and soft washing, or which one their house needs. A short card for each service helps:

  • House washing or soft washing: what surfaces you clean and how you protect plants and windows.
  • Driveways, sidewalks and patios.
  • Decks and fences, including whether you also seal or stain.
  • Roofs, gutters or commercial work, only if you offer them.

If you're insured, say so. Customers are trusting you with their siding, windows and landscaping.

Spell out your service area

List the towns or neighborhoods you serve. It saves you quote requests from too far away and helps search engines connect your site to local searches. Most pressure washing businesses don't have a storefront, so set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business. Google explains how to hide your address and list service areas.

Use reviews, the right way

Feature a few real reviews from customers and link to your Google profile for the rest. After each job, ask happy customers to leave a Google review. A follow-up text with your review link makes it easy. Don't write, buy or reward reviews: the FTC's rule banning fake reviews and testimonials covers businesses that do, and Google's policies prohibit fake and incentivized reviews.

Plan for the seasons

Pressure washing is seasonal in many areas. A website makes it easy to run a spring special or a holiday lighting add-on, if you offer one, without rebuilding anything. Keep one short promo line near the top of the page and swap it as the seasons change. Remove expired offers promptly; an old promo makes the whole site look abandoned.

Keep it fast and simple

Most people will see your site on a phone, often after tapping through from Facebook or Google. Large, uncompressed photos are the most common reason a gallery-heavy site is slow. Resize and compress your images, skip autoplay video, and check your page with Google's free PageSpeed Insights.

Local SEO basics for pressure washing

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Your site supports your Google listing when it:

  • has a clear title, like "Your Company | Pressure Washing in Your City";
  • names your services and towns in normal sentences;
  • uses the same business name and phone number as your Google profile and social pages;
  • includes local business structured data;
  • links to your Google profile, Facebook and Instagram so people can see more of your work.

Use your real business name on your profile. Google's guidelines don't allow stuffing extra keywords into it.

Keep social, but own your home base

Facebook and Instagram are great for showing new work. But you don't control how those platforms show your posts, and a feed is a hard place to compare prices and book. Use social to get attention and your website to close the job. Put your site link in every bio.

The short version

A pressure washing website that gets booked does a few things well: big before-and-after photos, a clear answer on price, a quote button that's always on screen, plain-language services, a defined service area and real reviews. Keep it fast and keep it current.
