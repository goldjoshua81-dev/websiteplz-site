When a homeowner's AC stops on a hot afternoon, they don't browse. They search, look at two or three companies and call one. Your website's job is to make sure that call is yours. This checklist covers what an HVAC website needs to do that, and what you can safely leave out.

1. A call button that never disappears

For no-heat and no-cool calls, the phone is everything. On a phone screen your number should be a tap-to-call link, at the top of the page, with a sticky call bar along the bottom so it stays visible while the homeowner scrolls. If you answer after hours or on weekends, say so right next to the button. If you don't, show your hours. Either way, nobody should have to hunt for the number.

Add a short book-a-visit form as well. Tune-ups, replacement quotes and second opinions are often not urgent, and some people prefer to send a request rather than call.

2. Services named the way homeowners search

Homeowners search for their problem: "AC not cooling," "furnace won't turn on," "new AC cost." Your site should use those words. Give your main services their own short sections, typically:

  • Repair: heating and cooling repair, what you work on and how fast you can usually get out.
  • Replacement and installs: the systems you install, and whether you offer free estimates.
  • Maintenance plans: what a plan includes and how to sign up. Plans create repeat customers, so give yours its own spot.

Only list what you actually do. If you don't do ductwork or commercial rooftop units, leave them off.

3. Trust signals above the fold

Homeowners are inviting you into their home and possibly spending real money on a new system. A strip of trust signals near the top of the page helps them decide quickly:

  • License and insurance. Show your license number as it appears on your license, and say you're insured if you are. Requirements vary by state.
  • Real reviews. Feature a few reviews from your own customers, with first names and towns if they agree. Never write, buy or reward reviews. The FTC banned fake reviews and testimonials in 2024, and Google's review policies prohibit fake and incentivized reviews.
  • Financing. If you offer financing for replacements, say so and link to your financing partner's application.
  • Your pricing approach. "Upfront pricing before we start" or "no extra charge for Saturdays" only if it's true. Clear promises beat vague ones.

4. Your service area, spelled out

List the cities, towns or counties you cover. It answers "do you come to me?" and helps search engines tie your site to local searches. If you work from home or a shop customers don't visit, Google lets service-area businesses hide their address and list service areas on their Business Profile. Make the website match the profile.

5. Real photos of your team and trucks

Stock photos of smiling models in clean uniforms don't build much trust. Photos of your actual techs, vans and installs do. They don't need to be professional. A clear, well-lit phone photo of your team in front of the truck works.

6. Speed on a phone

A homeowner standing in a hot house won't wait for a slow page. Keep the page light: compressed images, no autoplay video, no pile of plugins and pop-ups. You can test any page for free with Google's PageSpeed Insights, which also flags what's slowing it down.

7. Local SEO basics that support your Google listing

Your Google Business Profile does a lot of the work in local search. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Your website supports that when it:

  • has a clear page title, such as "Your Company | Heating & AC Repair in Your City" (Google explains how it uses titles);
  • describes your services and cities in real sentences;
  • uses the same business name, phone number and service area as your Google profile and other listings;
  • includes local business structured data, a small block of code that describes your business to search engines;
  • links to your Google profile so people can read more reviews.

Keep your profile name to your real business name. Google's guidelines don't allow extra keywords in the name.

8. Make it work with your ads

If you pay for Local Services Ads, Google Ads or lead services, many of those homeowners will check your website before they call. A page that matches what the ad promised, with the call button right there, helps you get value from leads you're already paying for.

What you can leave out

Most small HVAC companies don't need a 30-page site. Skip the blog you won't keep up, the "Our Philosophy" page and the sliders. A focused page with your services, trust signals, service area, reviews and a call button covers what homeowners need to decide.

Quick checklist

  • Tap-to-call number at the top and a sticky call bar
  • After-hours line or clear hours
  • Repair, replacement and maintenance-plan sections
  • License, insured and financing lines
  • A few real reviews
  • Your service area
  • Real photos of your team
  • Fast on a phone
  • Name, phone and area match your Google profile
  • A short book-a-visit form
