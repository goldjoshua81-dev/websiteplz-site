People looking for a house cleaner want to know three things quickly: what it will cost, whether they can trust you in their home, and how to book. Most cleaning businesses answer those questions one DM at a time. A good website answers them for everyone, around the clock. Here's what to put on it.

Make pricing clear, or at least easy to get

"How much for a three-bedroom?" is probably the question you answer most. Your website can take most of that off your plate:

  • Price by home size if your pricing works that way, for example by bedrooms and bathrooms.
  • Show the difference between clean types. Standard, deep and move-in or move-out cleans are priced differently, and customers often don't know which one they need. A sentence on each helps.
  • Show recurring options. If you offer weekly, every-other-week or monthly service at a lower rate, say so. Recurring clients are the steadiest part of most cleaning businesses.
  • Add a "Get My Price" form for anything that doesn't fit a simple table: bedrooms, bathrooms, type of clean, how often, and any extras like inside the fridge or oven.

If you'd rather not publish prices, a short form with a quick reply works too. Just don't leave people guessing.

Build trust before the first visit

Hiring a cleaner means letting someone into your home, sometimes when you're not there. The more your site answers "can I trust you?", the more inquiries turn into clients.

  • Insurance and bonding. If you carry liability insurance, say so. If you're bonded, say that too; a bond can protect clients if something goes wrong with an employee. Only list what you actually have.
  • Background checks. If you screen your team, say so. If you don't, don't imply it.
  • Your guarantee. A clear promise, such as a free re-clean of anything missed within a set time, reassures new clients. Word it exactly the way you'll honor it.
  • Real photos of your team. A friendly photo of you or your crew does more than a stock photo of a stranger with a mop.
  • Real reviews. Feature a few from your clients and link to your Google profile for more. Never write, buy or reward reviews. The FTC's rule banning fake reviews and testimonials covers businesses that do, and Google's review policies prohibit fake and incentivized reviews.

Make booking effortless

Many clients would rather book from their phone at 10 p.m. than call during the day. Give them a clear path:

  • A "Get My Price" or "Book" button at the top of the page that stays on screen as they scroll.
  • A tap-to-call or tap-to-text button for people who'd rather talk.
  • If you already use online booking software, link your Book button to it. If not, a short request form is fine.
  • Say what happens next: "We'll text you a price and available times."

Explain what's included

A simple checklist of what a standard clean covers, room by room, prevents surprises and complaints. Do the same for deep cleans and move-out cleans. It also shows you're organized, which matters to people trusting you with their home. If there are things you don't do, like windows outside or heavy lifting, say so kindly.

Show where you work

List the neighborhoods, towns or zip codes you cover. It cuts down on requests from too far away and helps search engines connect your site to local searches. Most cleaning businesses don't have a storefront, so set up your Google Business Profile as a service-area business. Google explains how to hide your address and list service areas.

Local SEO basics for cleaning businesses

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. Your site helps when it:

  • has a clear page title like "Your Company | House Cleaning in Your City" (Google explains how titles are used);
  • names your services and service area in normal sentences;
  • uses the same business name and phone number as your Google profile and social pages;
  • includes local business structured data;
  • loads quickly on a phone. Check it for free with PageSpeed Insights.

Keep asking happy clients for Google reviews, and reply to them. Use your real business name on your profile; Google's guidelines don't allow extra keywords in it.

Turn one-time cleans into regulars

Your site can do more than win the first booking. Mention your recurring options near your prices, explain any discount for regular service, and make it easy to ask for a recurring spot. A short line after the first clean, such as "Want this every other week?", in your follow-up text is a natural next step.

What you can skip

You don't need a big site. A single, warm, fast page with your prices or price form, trust signals, what's included, your service area, real reviews and an easy way to book covers what most clients need to decide. Skip the stock photos, pop-ups and pages you won't keep updated.

The short version

To get more cleaning clients from your website, answer price, trust and booking right on the page. Be specific, be honest about what you offer, and make the next step one tap away.
